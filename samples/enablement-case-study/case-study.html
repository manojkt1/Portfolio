Detailed Case Study

Reducing Inbound Support Tickets by ~30% via Self-Service Enablement

Executive Summary

By restructuring a fragmented set of developer documentation and product guides into structured, role-based learning paths and implementing a Docs-as-Code publishing workflow, we successfully deflected customer onboarding friction—resulting in a measurable 30% reduction in inbound helpdesk tickets within 6 months.

The Challenge

As the platform scaled to support over 100+ weekly releases, product resources became highly fragmented. Information was scattered across static wikis, PDF training manuals, and old release notes. Customers faced major friction points during initial API setups, prompting high-priority support tickets for basic configurations. Meanwhile, the customer success team spent hours repeating identical walkthroughs.

The Strategy

  1. Information Architecture Redesign: Conducted a complete content audit and mapped resources directly to three distinct target audiences: Administrators, Developers, and Customer Success Managers.
  2. Docs-as-Code Integration: Shifted documentation to a Git-backed Markdown pipeline, allowing engineers to submit documentation updates alongside code releases, ensuring Day 1 accuracy.
  3. Interactive Learning Portal: Designed a structured, role-based self-service training curriculum featuring quick-start guides, interactive code samples, and conceptual walk-throughs.

The Outcome & Impact

  • 30% Ticket Reduction: Setup-related tickets fell by approximately 30%, freeing the engineering and support teams to focus on complex, high-severity issues.
  • Self-Service Onboarding: Average partner onboarding time dropped from 3 weeks to under 5 days.
  • 100% Release Readiness: 100% of new feature releases shipped with complete, linted developer guides on the day of deploy.
